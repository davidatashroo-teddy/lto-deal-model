🧭 Before you run the numbers — two questions for the seller
  1. Do they own the home outright, or will they still be carrying a cost on it once the lease starts? If they still have a mortgage, they're paying it every month while you rent. That has to work for them or the deal doesn't happen.
  2. If they lease the home to you, where do they actually move to? If there's no clear answer, the lease-to-own isn't real yet — no matter how good the math looks.

Nothing below depends on these. They're just the gut check worth having before you start.

▶ How this calculator works — tap to expand

🏘 What's the deal?

Instead of buying a house today, you move in and pay rent for the lease term (12 months by default). At the end, you buy it at a pre-agreed price called the strike price. You also pay an upfront option fee that locks in your right to buy — think of it as a reservation deposit.

📊 What are we adding up?

For each scenario, we add up every dollar you'd spend over your full hold period: rent, down payment, closing costs, mortgage payments, property taxes, and insurance. That total is the lifetime cost — the true price of owning the home.

🔀 What's the breakeven price?

The most important number. It's the price at which buying a home outright today would cost you the exact same total as doing the deal. If comparable homes are priced above breakeven, the deal wins. Below breakeven, buying today wins.

📐 How is that calculated?

Every cost in a conventional purchase scales proportionally with the home price (taxes, insurance, mortgage, down payment). So we can solve for the exact breakeven price using algebra — no guessing required. Change any input and the breakeven updates instantly.
